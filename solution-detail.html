The Challenge

What a concert stage really demands

Touring consistency across venues

Problem

Venue dimensions, rigging positions and power conditions change from one tour stop to another.

Business impact

The production team may need extra programming time, additional crew and repeated fixture adjustments at every stop.

Solution response

Zone-based fixture groups, pre-addressed control files, labeled cabling and adaptable CAD layouts per venue.

Compressed programming & rehearsal time

Problem

Festival slots and tour stops leave hours — not days — for programming and focus.

Business impact

Overtime crew costs, delayed soundchecks and a first show that looks unfinished.

Solution response

Console-ready fixture personalities, pre-built groups and palettes, and busking-friendly show files delivered with the rig.

Camera, LED wall & audience see different things

Problem

Mixed fixture quality reads one way to the audience and another way on IMAG screens and broadcast cameras.

Business impact

Complaints from broadcast partners and recorded content the artist cannot reuse.

Solution response

Camera-facing zones use high-frequency PWM dimming, and CRI 90+ wash/profile fixtures are specified where skin tones matter. Final dimmer settings are confirmed against your target frame rate and shutter speed.

Rigging, power & transport constraints

Problem

Point loads, available power and truck space vary per venue and per country.

Business impact

On-site redesigns, locally rented distribution and load-in delays that cascade into show time.

Solution response

Load-referenced rigging drawings, per-zone power schedules and pre-cabled, labeled racks planned for transport volume.

FAQ

Concert lighting FAQ

What information is needed for a concert lighting plan?

Stage width and depth, trim height, venue type (indoor/outdoor), show dates, audience capacity, LED wall size, camera requirements, available power and preferred control protocol. Photos or a venue drawing speed things up.

How many stage lights are required?

It depends on stage size, trim height and throw distance. The mid-size example on this page uses roughly 60–80 fixtures across six zones; your zone plan states the final count per zone with the reasoning behind it.

Can the system work with an existing lighting console?

Yes. The system runs standard DMX, Art-Net and sACN, and fixture personalities are available for major console platforms. Your existing console can be integrated into the control plan.

How is the system adapted between tour stops?

Fixtures are grouped by zone, control files are pre-addressed, and cabling is labeled per zone. A venue change becomes a group edit and a CAD layout adjustment rather than a re-patch and re-program.

Which fixtures are suitable for camera recording?

Camera-facing zones use fixtures with high-frequency PWM dimming, and CRI 90+ wash or profile models are specified where skin-tone reproduction matters. Final dimmer settings are confirmed against your target frame rate and shutter speed; PWM frequency and camera notes are listed per fixture in the fixture schedule.

Can the package be used outdoors?

Yes. IP65-rated beam, PAR and strobe options cover outdoor stages, and the power and rigging plans are adjusted for weather exposure and wind-load requirements.

Do you provide rigging and power drawings?

Yes. The deliverables pack includes a rigging reference drawing with suspension points and load references, plus a per-zone power load schedule for your electrician.

What affects production and delivery time?

Fixture quantities, any customization, the testing schedule and the shipping method. A confirmed lead time is stated in the quotation, and packing follows the labeling plan so load-in starts immediately on arrival.
